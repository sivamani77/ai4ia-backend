We are living through an AI hype cycle.
Multiple corporations are promoting the advances
made thanks to transformer models,
as much as they are also promoting themselves
as a future of, well, almost anything.
In this hyping of generative AI, there has been two specific trends repeated constantly.
First of all, the notion of AI as potentially autonomous, outside of immediate and final
control by its creators, and from there, the notion of existential risk from potentially
conscious AI systems that may act against human interests.
Besides the exaggerations with little evidence behind, these two notions are basically arguments
based on the same arrangement, in which tech exists in a political and social vacuum.
A digital trend.
How to deal with this situation?
There are activists demanding justice in the allocation of resources,
not just in ecological terms, but also in the production of models themselves,
which are biased towards the western economies contained in industries,
showing biases in terms of class, ethnicity and gender, to name a few.
However, the current AI industry, as well as the whole digital media industry, is the
result of the realignment of capitalism in the late 20th century, when globalization
became a shortcut to describe a world open to all kinds of trade, where the competitive
advantages of some economies required the developing ones to let the innovation coming
from those developed world to enter freely into their local markets.
Under this scenario, the trend towards concentration of economic power in a few actors was a feature,
not a bug.
During the past decade, the consolidation of the dominant actors, most of them coming
from the USA, became a fact of life.
Even now, after some attempts to reign them in, countries like Canada or Australia have
faced the difficulties of setting national regulations into transnational private actors,
under current trade regimes.
The abandonment of news by Meta or Alphabet, for instance, is a result of decisions taken
by the actors from their own good, for their own good, notwithstanding the potential for
creating new deserts in countries where news information has turned mostly into a digital
game.
Without actual local alternatives, since the widespread adoption of digital media has overwhelmed
local media markets and fast destroyed conventional news outlets, urgent demand
for alternatives. A new regime has to accept that the current digital
ecosystem has to be reined in internationally, not just at the
national or regional level. This is particularly critical for emerging
economies. They lack the world with all to confront the power of transnational
corporations that exist in their digital realm, into which there are few, if any,
illegal instruments to act nationally. A fairer regime will not be able to provide the same
quality of services for every instance of use, because the internal structure of digital media,
including the pro-argument-sex AI, makes that impossible. We need accommodation to the local
without losing the power to understand the world and the global... cosmopolitan AI.
An actual cosmopolitan approach is needed. In this particular sense, cosmopolitan means
welcoming to diversity but also firming of identity. AI must have modes allowing fitting
limits to what can be used but also of what should be used, while maintaining a level of openness
to the world with its whole richness of human knowledge. This must be done respecting intellectual
property rights but also of firming fair use and promoting linguistic and cultural independence
without losing the power of inputting diversity.

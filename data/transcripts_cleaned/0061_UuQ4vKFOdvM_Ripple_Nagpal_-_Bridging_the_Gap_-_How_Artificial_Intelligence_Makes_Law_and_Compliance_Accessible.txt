Let me share how this works in my daily life professional life.
First, drafting and reviewing agreements.
A typical shareholders agreement can run over a hundred pages.
Traditionally, a lawyer or an associate would take days to read it, line by line, identifying
what's unusual, what's risky, and what could trap the client.
Today, AI allows me to summarize such documents in minutes, highlighting clauses that deviate
from standard practice.
For example, in one transaction, AI instantly flagged an obscure line that shifted liability
entirely onto the founder in case of tax claims.
It would have taken hours for a human associate to catch it.
AI did not make the decision for me, but it gave me the flashlight to see the risk clearly.
I still had to argue, negotiate and protect my clients interests, but I did it with better
speed and sharper focus.
2. Compliance management In India, compliance is like a daily puzzle.
Every company must file dozens of returns with the registrar of companies, the income
tax department, the RBI and other regulators. Missing just one can mean penalties. Traditionally,
this meant frantic calls, thick compliance calendars and the constant fear of a deadline
slipping through the cracks. With AI, I now build systems that track these obligations,
send timely reminders and explain requirements in plain English. A young founder can receive
a simple AI-generated note, your company must file form MGT-7 this month. This is your annual return,
a snapshot of your company's shareholding and governance and that's it. No jargon, no confusion,
just clarity. This is information accessibility in its purest sense. Third, new diligence. In the
world of investments, new diligence is key but it is also the most exhausting part of legal work.
Traditionally, teams of lawyers would spend weeks buried in documents, scanning leases,
employment contracts, and board minutes to identify hidden risks.
With AI, I can upload entire data rooms and receive a structured summary of red flags
in hours.
I recall a case where AI highlighted that a critical intellectual property license was
missing renewal.
catching that early saved my client both money and future litigation. This does
mean AI replaces my, this does not mean that AI replaces my judgment. It
amplifies it. Instead of drowning in paper, I can swim straight to the insights.
Fourth, client education. Many of my clients are startup founders, often
brilliant technologies but new to the world of law. Terms like ESOP, liquidation preference,
convertible notes sound alien to them. In the past, I would spend hours simplifying
these concepts, often repeating myself across different clients. Now I use AI to create guides
that explain these concepts in everyday language. For example, I once asked AI to explain liquidation
preference in the style of a cricket analogy. And it worked. The founder finally understood
that investors like batsmen with extra life get to recover their money before anyone else does.
That moment of clarity was priceless. Fifth, knowledge management. Law firms, even small ones,
generate a mountain of precedents, templates and advisory notes. Searching through them
was once painful. With AI powered search, I can find exactly what I need in seconds.
A client asks, how did we drop the non-compete clause in the last deal last
year? In the past I would send an intern into the abyss of files. Now I simply ask
AI and it retrieves the precedents instantly. Efficiency is not just about
saving time. It is about ensuring no knowledge ever goes unused.
Sixth, strategy and decision making. Beyond the mechanical tasks, AI helps me think strategically
by analyzing trends in judgments or regulatory updates. It helps me anticipate how laws are
revolving. For example, when Indian courts started leaning towards stricter enforcement
of shareholder rights, AI tool helped me map that trend quickly. This allowed me to advise
my clients to renegotiate their contracts proactively. Accessibility here is not just
about understanding today's law, but about peeking into tomorrow's direction.
Now, let me also share some humor, because lawyers needed more than anyone. I often tell

We deal with sensitive data, contracts, financials, shareholder disputes.
Using AI does not mean abandoning confidentiality.
It means being careful, anonymizing data and ensuring that machines never substitute human

Accessibility without trust is dangerous.

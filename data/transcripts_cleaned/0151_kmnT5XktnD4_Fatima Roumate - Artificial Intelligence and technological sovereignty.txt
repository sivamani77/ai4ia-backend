Data sovereignty means that the state is able to regulate, manage, and protect the data
generated within or passing through their territories, and take measures towards effective regulation
of data based on international law. Data sovereignty is important, especially with the increasing
use of drones which is a risk to privacy and security. There are currently 1.7 million drones

government, companies, terrorists, and individuals. At least 143 countries have enacted some form of

Cyberspace Sovereignty
Cyber sovereignty means autonomy in cyberspace.
In the same context, the United Nations governmental group of experts decided that international
law, including state sovereignty, was applicable in cyberspace.
The applicability of international law and state sovereignty to cyberspace was also confirmed
by the Tallinn Manual on the International Law Applicable to Cyber Warfare.
Technology Sovereignty
Jacob Edler et al. defined a technology sovereignty as the ability of a state or a Federation of
States to provide the technologies it deems critical for its welfare, competitiveness,
and ability to act, and to be able to develop these or source them from other economic areas
without one-sided structural dependency.
Sovereignty in international public law means independence, which is now threatened by AI,
which explains the importance of the extension of sovereignty to cyberspace.
However, cyberspace sovereignty is conditioned by technological sovereignty, which is a large
notion that refers to other types of sovereignty such as innovation and scientific sovereignty.
Technological sovereignty refers to technology independence in all fields, politics, economy,
and society.
This means states should be able to choose their political and economic systems without
any intervention or influence from other states such as the use of AI to manipulate public
opinion and.
to change election results. AI is influencing several sectors, including education, health
services, and the labor market. Therefore, technological sovereignty depends on other
types of sovereignty, for example, innovation and scientific sovereignty. Technological
sovereignty is a condition to sovereignty as a principle of international law, and it's
a key to other types of sovereignty. This explains the race and competition for AI and
technological sovereignty. Competition between states for technological
sovereign could be illustrated using three criteria. AI and robotics investment,

Source: FinalAI4IA-2021, AI4IA-2022, 2023-AI4IA, 393721eng (AI4IA 2024), and AI4IA-Conference-Report-2025 PDFs. Names were extracted from each report's session/theme/satellite-event listings, normalized (honorifics stripped, known spelling variants merged — e.g. "Erik Langner"/"Eric Langer", "Marielza Oliveira"/"Oliviera"), then deduplicated by name across all five years. Individuals credited only as conference organizers, drafting-team members or volunteers (with no recorded talk) are excluded. Given inconsistent formatting across five independently-produced reports, treat the unique count as a close estimate rather than an audited figure.

Video links: matched programmatically against the 291 uploads across all 18 playlists on the AI4IA Conference YouTube channel, by comparing each video's title (usually "Speaker Name – Talk Title") against the speaker name and topic from the report, with regional satellite-event videos matched by keyword where no individual upload exists. 318 of 383 topic entries have at least one matched video, shown with the upload's duration and view count pulled directly from YouTube. The rest were either never uploaded individually, folded into a multi-speaker panel recording the matcher couldn't confidently attribute, or the title didn't contain enough information to match reliably. Verify a link before citing it if precision matters — this is an automated best-effort match, not a manual audit.

Affiliation: the speaker's title/organization exactly as stated in the conference report at the time they spoke (not necessarily their current role); shown for the 187 speakers whose report entry included one. Where a report gave more than one credit (e.g. multiple roles), all are kept.

Correction: an earlier pass of this register had merged "Andrew Knight" (2022, RICS, UK — built-environment/surveying) with "Andy Knight" (2025, Faculty of Arts, University of Alberta) as one person on name similarity alone. Pulling their affiliations surfaced that these are almost certainly two different people, so they're now listed separately — the unique-speaker count moved from 328 to 329 accordingly.
